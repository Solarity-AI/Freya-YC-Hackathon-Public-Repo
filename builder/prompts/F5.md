Facet F5: intents (optionally interpretation overrides). Situations, outcomes, notes and multi-product rules are
extracted by another call (facet F7): refer to situations only by the shared situation ids.

- intents: one per thing a customer or third party may say that the brief gives a rule for, plus generic ones the
  rules need (robot/AI question, scam question, wants a human, does not want to talk, asks to change a recorded
  promise, asks for a forbidden offer, full amount too much). Each intent:
  id (snake_case), label, description (one sentence), examples (exactly 2 short realistic customer utterances, in the call language), negatives
  (exactly 1 near miss that must NOT trigger it), keywords (Python regexes, case-insensitive, run on the customer's
  words as a backstop; in a call language other than English give them in that language AND in English, since
  they are matched on the customer's own words and on an English rendering of them; for a language with endings
  match the stem and let the ending run on (e.g. \bborc\w* or \bbor[cç]\w*); use \b word boundaries; keep them specific; allow the usual words in between, e.g.
  call( \w+)? back). A keyword must name the intent itself, not merely a word often said with it (a bare "agent",
  "computer", "sick" or "died" also fires on "my advisor said...", "I'm not sick", "her husband died"), scope (stages where it applies: rp = before
  the right party is confirmed, tp = third party, id = identity questions, neg = negotiation, conf = read-back),
  precedence (0-1000, higher wins when two fire in one turn), product_scoped (true if it concerns one product),
  action: one of
  * answer_continue {say}: answer and repeat the pending question (robot, scam, forbidden offer, change request).
  * resolve_product {say, situation}: the product ends in that situation (paid, dispute).
  * transfer {say, situation, queue, keep_recorded, drop_unrecorded, suppress_notes}: transfer now.
  * ask_then_transfer {question, say, situation, queue}: first ask whether the agent can help; transfer if insisted.
  * offer_transfer {say, queue, accept_situation, decline_situation, suspend_persuasion, accept_say}: sympathy +
    offer of a transfer (hardship); say = one short sympathy sentence, then the offer question as the LAST sentence
    (the question alone is repeated if the customer does not answer). accept_say: what the agent says when the
    customer accepts, announcing the transfer (e.g. "I will connect you with <team> now."). yes -> transfer,
    no -> decline_situation.
  * count_then_close {say, limit, situation, situation_pre_verification}: e.g. "says twice they do not want to
    talk" (limit 2); before verification it closes with situation_pre_verification. say = a short acknowledgement
    said on an earlier occurrence before the pending question is asked again; never a goodbye (the goodbye is
    added when the call closes). Scope id/neg/conf: the right
    party must be confirmed first (transfer kinds likewise never apply in rp/tp).
  * close_now {say, situation}: e.g. wrong number, deceased (scope rp/tp).
  * too_much {}: the full amount is too much (handled by the negotiation code).
  Disambiguate overlapping intents with negatives and precedence: completed payment (a past-tense payment claim) vs a future
  payment (a proposal); a claim the product was closed or paid off in the past is a dispute when the brief says so;
  hardship needs one of the causes the brief lists; a bare "I can't afford it" is too_much. Pre-verification scopes (rp, tp, id) must not get lines with banned words.
- interpretation (optional): only the keys the brief explicitly decides differently from the generic defaults.
  after_record: "ask_anything_else" when the brief lets the customer go on after a recorded promise (a transfer or
  a person request that follows a recorded promise, a request to change a recorded promise, "at any point"); else
  "close".
